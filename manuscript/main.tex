\begin{abstract}
An Internet of Things (IoT) target may have trusted benign observations but no labeled attacks, while supervised models trained at its peers provide attack evidence. PACE (Peer-wise Calibrated Experts) combines frozen supervised peer classifiers with a protected local anchor and a heterogeneous anomaly-expert bank. Each peer receives a target-relative threshold, while feature-rarity and peer-response experts detect deviations from target benign behavior. We evaluate 58 targets across six corpora and seven study strata, using three learning seeds and local fitting budgets of 100 and 400 rows. With the peer branch fixed, replacing a seven-detector mean local ensemble improves target-macro detection by 2.34 and 2.32 percentage points at nominal $\alpha=0.05$. Mean realized false-positive rates (FPRs) decrease from 5.74\% to 5.55\% and from 6.44\% to 5.43\%, respectively. At $\alpha=0.01$, detection gains of 2.61 and 2.83 points accompany higher mean FPRs. Rarity-family removal produces the largest loss among the tested expert-family ablations. Gains concentrate in TON\_IoT, with smaller gains elsewhere; local experts add detections when peers miss attacks. A separate comparison fixes the complete local branch: peer-wise decisions improve detection by 3.0--7.3 points over mean or maximum peer fusion, with higher mean FPR. The results support complementary peer and local detection on this benchmark, which also informed development. Coarse operating-point sweeps do not establish general equal-FPR superiority, and empirical calibration does not guarantee future false-alert control.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} IoT intrusion detection; peer models; benign-only learning; empirical calibration; anomaly ensembles; false alerts.
