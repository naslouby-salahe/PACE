\begin{abstract}
Detecting intrusions at a newly monitored IoT target is difficult when only benign target observations are available but peer clients possess labeled attacks. Combining peer scores under a single calibrated threshold can obscure useful evidence from individual classifiers. PACE retains separately calibrated peer evidence and combines the resulting alert union with a protected local anchor and a heterogeneous bank of anomaly experts. The local branch incorporates feature-rarity and peer-response evidence without using target attack labels. We evaluate PACE on 58 targets spanning six corpora and seven study strata, using three learning seeds and local fitting budgets of 100 and 400 rows. Relative to an identical peer branch paired with a seven-detector mean local ensemble, PACE improves target-macro detection by 2.61 and 2.83 percentage points at nominal $\alpha=0.01$, and by 2.34 and 2.32 points at $\alpha=0.05$. At the latter operating parameter, PACE also reduces mean realized false-alert rates at both fitting budgets. Ablations identify rarity-based evidence as the largest measured complementary contribution among the tested expert families. Gains are strongest in TON\_IoT, with smaller improvements elsewhere. False-alert rates remain heterogeneous across targets. With the complete local branch held fixed, retaining separately thresholded peers improves detection by 3.0--7.3 percentage points over mean or maximum peer-score fusion at the tested nominal settings, with higher mean false-alert rates. PACE's gains therefore reflect complementary peer and local detection rather than a guarantee of future false-alert control.
\end{abstract}
