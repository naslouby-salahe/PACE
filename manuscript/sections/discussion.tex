\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Complementary local coverage}
The local branch adds detections only where the peer union misses attacks. Local-exclusive coverage reaches 14.74\% in TON\_IoT and 12.10\% in IoT-23 at $\alpha=0.01$, $N=400$, but is nearly zero in N-BaIoT. Improved standalone local detection therefore need not produce proportional end-to-end gains.

The rarity-family removals produce the largest detection losses among the tested expert-family ablations in all four primary configurations. This supports the usefulness of marginal and pairwise feature-rarity evidence alongside the shape- and neighborhood-based detectors. Removing the response expert causes smaller losses in final detection. Because each removal recalibrates the remaining union, the losses characterize complete ablated rules rather than an additive attribution to individual experts.

The protected anchor preserves an averaged local decision path alongside sparse expert alerts. At $\alpha=0.05$, removing it and reallocating its share to the bank raises both detection and false alerts. Protection therefore refers to the separate decision path, not to guaranteed non-inferiority. The primary comparison changes the comparator's seven-member composition and thresholding, so the overall gain belongs to the complete local-branch replacement; the ablations do not establish that every component is necessary.

\subsection{Retaining peer evidence separately}
Peer-wise calibration gives each classifier its own target-relative threshold. Unlike a threshold on a fused maximum, a high threshold for one peer need not directly exclude another peer's smaller evidence. This structure addresses the proposed peer-drowning failure mode while retaining dependence through the common union multiplier; the aggregate outcomes do not isolate that mechanism.

The fixed-local-branch comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:peer-fusion} show that separate peer decisions recover additional detections at a higher mean FPR. This is a distinct result from the primary local-branch improvement. The clamped sweep estimates in Section~\ref{sec:sweep-adjustment} concern the mean-local comparator and cannot resolve the equal-FPR comparison with the peer-fusion arms.

The shared multiplier uses overlap between calibration alerts to relax thresholds while checking their union on every block. It does not require independent peer alerts. It also couples the retained thresholds, since changing one expert's calibration alerts can change the multiplier for all peers. These constraints apply to observed calibration blocks, not to future traffic.

\subsection{Variation across targets and fitting budgets}
The coverage decomposition explains where local detection can help, without establishing why particular corpora offer more opportunities. Target-level and exclusion analyses show that gains need not be uniform. More fitting data can change the detected deviations, but only detections outside peer coverage improve the final OR. The fixed-cohort comparisons, including the adverse high-resource result, provide no evidence of monotonic end-to-end benefit.

\subsection{Practical interpretation}
At $\alpha=0.05$, PACE's primary detection gains accompany lower mean FPR at both budgets. This joint improvement supports the heterogeneous local branch within the evaluated benchmark. Operational assessment must additionally consider the target distribution: at $\alpha=0.01$, medians below 0.01 coexist with means above 0.03 and worst-target rates near 0.67. The upper tail remains a deployment limitation even when average performance improves.

Applying the detector requires trusted benign onboarding data for fitting, reference construction, and calibration, plus the preprocessing access in Table~\ref{tab:data-access}. References and thresholds remain fixed in the evaluated rule. Independent validation would need to establish when to update them, how to respond to excessive alerts, and how any recalibration changes detection.

Row alerts also require a policy for aggregation into actionable incidents. An anomalous record need not represent an actionable security event~\cite{sommer2010closed}, and precision depends on attack prevalence~\cite{saito2015precision}. Apruzzese's operational analysis emphasizes the aggregation and triage of reports beyond individual flagged records~\cite{apruzzese2026sok}. The benchmark's separately sampled classes and target-macro rates do not measure incident precision or analyst workload. Consequently, higher detection on this benchmark does not by itself establish a lower operational workload.
