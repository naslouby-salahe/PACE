\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Complementarity is more informative than local accuracy}
The branch decomposition explains why a larger fitting budget need not substantially improve the combined detector. Under an OR rule, additional local detections matter only when the peer branch would otherwise remain silent. Improving local detection on already detected attacks does not change the final decision. The observed local-exclusive coverage in TON\_IoT and IoT-23 is therefore relevant to the architecture, while near-zero exclusive coverage in N-BaIoT limits the opportunity for improvement there.

This interpretation does not establish which expert caused each gain. Removing an expert family also changes the calibration of the remaining bank, and the primary comparator changes both detector composition and thresholding. The ablations support complementarity at the level of the tested configurations. They cannot identify a unique causal contribution for every detector or justify adding individual removal costs together. In particular, the response-space expert has a small marginal effect; presenting it as the dominant explanation for the primary gain would overstate the evidence.

The anchor similarly serves an empirical design purpose rather than a theorem. Its separate path can preserve useful averaged evidence when sparse bank experts fail, but it excludes the comparator's autoencoder and receives different calibration. The scenario-12 loss demonstrates that preserving an anchor does not preserve every target decision of the comparator. At the larger nominal parameter, removing the anchor actually increases aggregate detection along with false alerts. The anchor's value must therefore be discussed as an operating tradeoff within the evaluated rule.

\subsection{What peer-wise calibration does and does not solve}
Keeping peers separate prevents a single fused-score threshold from being the sole route for their evidence. The peer-mean and peer-maximum ablations, which retain the local bank, provide a more direct architectural comparison than the separately recorded probability-based max-fusion baseline. Their lower nominal detection supports preserving separate experts in these experiments, but their lower false-alert rates also prevent interpreting the full detection difference as a pure improvement at equal operational cost.

The joint multiplier is an adaptive use of the calibration sample. It exploits overlap between expert alerts to loosen individually conservative thresholds while checking the union on the same blocks. This is a practical empirical procedure. It neither makes peers statistically independent nor removes distribution shift. The shared multiplier also means that peer-wise thresholding does not completely isolate experts from each other: changes in one expert's calibration alerts can alter the retained multiplier for the whole union.

The benign-tail extrapolation extends score resolution beyond the reference maximum. That extension can preserve distinctions between unusually large scores, but its numerical output is not a calibrated probability in the extrapolated region. A formal conformal or multiple-testing guarantee would require assumptions and a rule different from merely assigning a familiar interpretation to these evidence values. This paper consequently reports the actual decision rule and its measured errors without importing guarantees from superficially similar rank procedures.

\subsection{Operational meaning of calibration failure}
False-alert heterogeneity is central to the deployment assessment. At nominal 0.01, a median target rate below 0.01 coexists with a mean above 0.03 and a worst target near 0.67. A service that must monitor every target cannot treat the median as evidence that all targets are adequately calibrated. Conversely, the worst target does not imply identical failure everywhere. The full distribution is needed to distinguish broad performance from concentrated failure.

Operational decisions would require an independently validated policy for collecting trusted benign data, detecting changing traffic conditions, updating references or models, and handling excessive alerts. None of those policies is evaluated here. Recalibration might reduce false alerts but could also remove detections, so it cannot be assumed to preserve the reported gain. Turning row decisions into actionable incidents would additionally require temporal grouping and an alert-handling policy. The current results offer no measured incident precision or analyst workload.

The operational distinction is well established: an anomalous observation is not necessarily an actionable security incident, and the cost of errors depends on the monitoring context~\cite{sommer2010closed}. Precision additionally depends on attack prevalence~\cite{saito2015precision}. Because the benchmark samples benign and attack roles separately and aggregates target rates, those macro rates cannot be converted into a measured deployment precision or analyst workload. The present analysis therefore evaluates discrimination and false alerts within the retained data, rather than asserting an operational benefit of a particular magnitude.

\subsection{The defensible scope of the contribution}
The strongest supported claim is an empirical improvement over the specified mean-ensemble local comparator in the universal primary cohort. Gains persist in the repository's approximate operating-point analysis and in pooled summaries excluding TON\_IoT, but they are smaller outside that corpus. The 2000- and 20,000-row studies further limit any claim that the bank increasingly benefits from more local fitting data.

PACE is not federated learning: no iterative parameter aggregation or collaborative optimization is part of its decision architecture. Separately supplied peer models could reduce the need to transfer raw examples at detection time, but the benchmark's pooled preprocessing and centralized execution are not measurements of such a deployment. Privacy, communication efficiency, and protection against malicious peers remain untested. The architecture should therefore be assessed as an offline model-combination study whose operational requirements have yet to be established.
