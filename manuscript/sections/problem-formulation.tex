\section{Problem formulation} 
\label{sec:problem} 
Each benchmarked client is referred to as a target: it contributes benign rows for which no attack labels exist in the relevant client detector(s). This may correspond to a single physical device/host or an entire capture, depending on the corpus. Thus each client need not correspond one-to-one to a distinct physical system, and instead "target" is used as a generic label. Clients/targets have a common feature set amongst their eligible peer group per experiment corpus/subset, and models trained on mutually-incompatible features will not be transferred.

$T_N$ denotes the benign sequence of length $N$ corresponding to a target's training role (of those held out for this purpose), $R$ its benign validation/reserve role, and $C$ the target's corresponding calibration role. Finally, there is additionally a benign test role and unseen attacks against the target for evaluation purposes. For peer $k$, a learned detector of the form $m_k: x \mapsto \ell_{atk}(x)-\ell_{ben}(x)$ is assumed to be available, returning the difference between logits for the positive (attack) and negative class respectively. The client detector corresponding to each target (when each target also serves as a peer in a different context) is excluded. The goal is to exploit learnt attack knowledge from peers and complement with an unsupervised detector fit on the target's local benign sequences ($T_N$), in the absence of any labelled attack data for the target.

Given a threshold/alert rule $A_t$ applied to $\mathcal{X}_t$, the following TPR / FPR pairs are considered:
\begin{equation} 
\operatorname{TPR}_t=\Pr(A_t(X) = 1 | Y=1, t),\quad
\operatorname{FPR}_t=\Pr(A_t(X) = 1 | Y=0, t),
\label{eq:objective} 
\end{equation} 
where a higher TPR at tolerable FPR is preferred. Note that while these quantities motivate the method design, they do not directly imply any particular constrained optimisation problem is solved due to the absence of attacks, and further because no strict statistical guarantees are provided regarding FPR for subsequent observations based on $\alpha$ (see below). Empirical measurements of average TPR/FPR against retained benign/attacks over all attack classes, clients/targets and random initialisations follow Section~\ref{sec:experiment}.

Note that $N$, the target benign local fit size, need not represent all of the available target observations to support the experiment. Further benign reserve and calibration observations are also required. Moreover, during execution, the preprocessors fit the mean/scale vectors based on the full available set of client training observations, i.e.\ including some of the target rows beyond the prefix $T_N$. The experiments thus vary $N$ in terms of the target-specific detector fit size only, with fixed amounts of further benign target validation/calibration and pooled client preprocessing. The findings therefore should not be read as characterising behaviour with only $N$ available samples for each target.

Let $\alpha$ be the operating threshold and $r$ be the local reserve fraction, with corresponding peer/local budget split $B_P=(1-r)\alpha, B_L=r\alpha$. None of $\{B_P,B_L,\alpha\}$ are intended to be an upper-bound on expected FPR of unseen traffic at runtime (though see the discussion of thresholds for each local/peer score below, where the combined scores' threshold can exceed $\alpha$ in aggregate). Known, clean target observations; reliable pretrained peer classifiers; and appropriate feature alignment across the corpus are assumed (assumptions/limitations elaborated in Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}). Benchmark clients are pre-processed in bulk.
