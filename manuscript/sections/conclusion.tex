\section{Conclusion}
PACE combines separately calibrated supervised peer classifiers with a protected local anchor and a heterogeneous bank of anomaly experts for targets that provide benign observations but no attack labels. Across 58 benchmark targets, replacing a seven-detector mean local branch improves target-macro detection in all four primary configurations. The gains range from approximately 2.32 to 2.83 percentage points, and at nominal $\alpha=0.05$ they accompany lower mean realized false-alert rates. Rarity-based local evidence supplies the largest measured contribution among the tested expert-family removals, while local-exclusive coverage explains where additional anomaly experts can improve the combined decision.

The results also identify the method's operating boundaries. Gains are concentrated in TON\_IoT, become smaller in several other strata, and do not consistently increase with larger local fitting budgets. Realized false-alert rates remain heterogeneous, and the empirical calibration rule does not establish uniform test-time FPR control. The experiments support complementary peer and local detection when the target provides benign data without attack labels. The principal benefit is the additional coverage obtained from separately retained peer decisions and a heterogeneous local branch; operational deployment still requires independent false-alert validation.
