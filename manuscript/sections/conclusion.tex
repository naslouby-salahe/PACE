\section{Conclusion}
PACE combines separately target-calibrated supervised peers with a protected anchor and a heterogeneous benign-only local bank. In the 58-target universal primary cohort, the local-branch replacement improves mean detection over the mean-ensemble comparator by approximately 0.023--0.028 across the two nominal operating parameters and two fitting budgets. The benefit is concentrated in TON\_IoT, with smaller pooled gains elsewhere, little change in several strata, and a measurable target regression. Larger fitting budgets do not yield a consistent additional advantage.

The method remains an empirical calibration procedure. Its low-alpha resolution rule does not strictly partition the nominal allocation, and realized false-alert rates are both higher and more heterogeneous than a nominal guarantee would suggest. These results support further study of complementary peer and local evidence under explicit data-access assumptions. They do not establish uniform false-alert control, privacy, adversarial robustness, or readiness for unattended deployment.
