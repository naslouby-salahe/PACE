\section{Conclusion}
PACE combines separately calibrated supervised peers with a protected local anchor and a heterogeneous anomaly-expert bank under benign-only target fitting and calibration. Across 58 benchmark targets, replacing a seven-detector mean local branch improves target-macro detection by 2.32--2.83 percentage points in all four primary configurations. At nominal $\alpha=0.05$, these gains accompany lower mean realized false-alert rates. Rarity-family removal produces the largest loss among the tested expert-family ablations; local-exclusive coverage identifies detections beyond the peer union. Separately retained peer decisions improve detection over mean or maximum peer fusion, with higher mean FPR.

The evidence supports complementary peer and local detection within this benchmark. Gains concentrate in TON\_IoT and do not consistently increase with fitting budget. Development-cohort reuse and heterogeneous false-alert rates limit generalization. Validation on untouched traffic is needed to determine whether the detection gains persist and false-alert rates are acceptable in deployment.
